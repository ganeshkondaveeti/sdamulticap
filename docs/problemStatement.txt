1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Network engineers troubleshooting packet loss, latency, misrouting, onboarding
failures or protocol errors across enterprise and service-provider networks
built from Cisco IOS-XE Catalyst 9000 switches, Cisco Catalyst 9800 Wireless LAN
Controllers, classic Cisco IOS devices, Cisco ASR and ISR routers, and Cisco
Nexus (NX-OS) switches have no unified way to capture traffic at multiple points
in the network AT THE SAME TIME.

Each Cisco operating system exposes a different, mutually incompatible capture
mechanism. IOS-XE and IOS-XR provide Embedded Packet Capture (EPC). Many classic
Catalyst Layer 2 platforms provide no on-box capture at all and require
SPAN/RSPAN. NX-OS provides no EPC equivalent: ethanalyzer captures only
CPU-punted control-plane traffic and is a supervisor-CPU risk if used without a
capture filter, so data-plane visibility on Nexus requires SPAN or ERSPAN.

The introduction of wireless makes the problem qualitatively harder, not merely
broader. Three distinct capture domains must be reconciled:

  (a) WIRED domain - conventional frames on switch and router interfaces.
  (b) CAPWAP TUNNEL domain - in central-switching mode, wireless client traffic
      traverses the wired network encapsulated in CAPWAP (UDP 5246 control,
      UDP 5247 data). A capture taken anywhere between the access point and the
      Catalyst 9800 shows only the tunnel, not the client traffic, and is
      unusable without automated decapsulation and inner-flow correlation.
  (c) OVER-THE-AIR (802.11) domain - association, authentication, EAP exchange,
      key negotiation, retries, roaming, RSSI and channel behaviour are
      invisible to every wired capture. Observing them requires placing an
      access point into sniffer mode, which removes that access point from
      service and disconnects its clients, and which streams 802.11 frames to a
      remote listener using PEEKREMOTE encapsulation rather than writing a file.

Furthermore, wireless deployment models break the assumption that traffic
follows a single discoverable wired path. Under FlexConnect local switching, or
under SD-Access fabric-enabled wireless, client data traffic does not traverse
the Catalyst 9800 at all - it is switched locally at the access point or
VXLAN-tunneled to the fabric edge. A tool that captures on the controller and
reports "no traffic observed" in these modes is not merely unhelpful, it is
actively misleading.

As a result, engineers today must manually determine the switching mode and
traffic path, log in to each device individually, recall five or more different
command syntaxes across wired and wireless platforms, start captures
sequentially - guaranteeing that no two captures cover the same instant of the
same transient event - manually transfer capture files off each device, manually
decapsulate CAPWAP, manually correlate an over-the-air capture against a wired
capture against controller-side client debug output, and manually reconcile
capture files whose device clocks are not aligned. This is slow, error prone,
frequently fails on the first attempt because the event does not reoccur, and
leaves residual capture configuration, ACLs, monitor sessions and out-of-service
sniffer-mode access points on production infrastructure when sessions are
interrupted.

No tool exists that (a) discovers the relevant Cisco wired and wireless devices
and their switching modes, (b) automatically selects the correct capture
mechanism per platform and per capture domain, (c) arms and triggers all
captures near-simultaneously, (d) enforces safety guardrails to protect
production control planes and wireless service availability, (e) guarantees
removal of all temporary device configuration and restoration of all access
point operating modes, and (f) returns a single time-aligned, cross-domain
correlated packet set that localizes the fault to a specific device, interface,
radio or protocol exchange.

2. IMPACT OF THE PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Mean time to resolution for intermittent, path-dependent and client-specific
  faults is measured in days, because each capture attempt is a fresh manual
  coordination exercise.
- Wireless client onboarding failures are especially costly to diagnose because
  the failure evidence is split across three capture domains that no engineer
  can currently capture simultaneously.
- Transient faults are routinely missed entirely due to non-simultaneous capture.
- Evidence quality is low: unaligned timestamps and undecapsulated CAPWAP make
  merged captures unusable or actively misleading.
- Production risk is introduced by unfiltered captures (Nexus supervisor CPU
  spikes, exhausted control-plane memory, oversubscribed SPAN destinations) and
  by access points left in sniffer mode after a failed capture attempt.
- Deep, per-platform expertise across both wired and wireless domains is
  required, so the work cannot be delegated to first-line operations staff.

3. OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Deliver a cross-platform desktop application - distributed as a signed .exe for
Microsoft Windows, a signed and notarized .dmg for macOS, and .deb and AppImage
packages for Ubuntu Linux - that discovers Cisco wired and wireless network
devices and orchestrates safe, synchronized, multi-device, multi-domain packet
captures, returning a single correlated, time-aligned capture set with an
automated fault-localization verdict.

The flagship capability is a single client-centric intent: given one wireless
client MAC address, the tool simultaneously captures over-the-air 802.11 frames,
CAPWAP tunnel traffic, controller-side client state, and the wired forwarding
path, and presents them as one correlated timeline.

4. SCOPE - IN SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Supported platform families
    - IOS-XE switching: Catalyst 9200, 9300, 9400, 9500, 9600.
    - IOS-XE wireless: Catalyst 9800-L, 9800-40, 9800-80, 9800-CL, and Embedded
      Wireless Controller on Catalyst 9000 switches, including HA SSO pairs.
    - IOS-XE routing: ASR 1000, ISR 4000, Catalyst 8000, CSR 1000v.
    - Classic Cisco IOS: ISR G2 and legacy Catalyst platforms.
    - IOS-XR: ASR 9000.
    - NX-OS: Nexus 3000, 5000, 7000, 9000.
    - Cisco access points operating with a Catalyst 9800 controller, including
      local, FlexConnect, fabric and sniffer modes.

4.2 Device and topology discovery
    - Seed-based CDP/LLDP topology crawl with hop limit and CIDR allow-list.
    - SNMP fingerprinting (sysObjectID, ENTITY-MIB, LLDP-MIB).
    - Inventory ingest from Cisco Catalyst Center / DNA Center, Prime
      Infrastructure, NSO, Nexus Dashboard and APIC where available.
    - Subnet sweep with SSH/NETCONF capability probing.
    - Platform, OS family and software release classification.
    - Wireless topology discovery: controllers, HA role (active/standby), access
      point inventory, AP-to-switch attachment point, radio and channel
      assignment, WLAN and policy profiles, and per-WLAN switching mode
      (central switching, FlexConnect local switching, fabric-enabled).
    - Client location resolution: given a client MAC or IP, identify the
      associated AP, radio, band, controller, WLAN, VLAN and wired uplink.
    - L2/L3 path computation between a source and destination, extended to
      include the wireless segment and CAPWAP tunnel endpoints.

4.3 Capture capability abstraction
    - Per-platform driver plugins with a common contract:
      probe, arm, start, stop, fetch, cleanup.
    - Ranked strategy selection per device: native on-box capture, then ERSPAN
      to a built-in local collector, then local SPAN to a designated capture
      host, then explicit declaration of a coverage gap.
    - Catalyst 9800 specific mechanisms: Embedded Packet Capture on wired and
      wireless interfaces, control-plane capture, CAPWAP inner-filter capture
      to match encapsulated client traffic directly, access point packet
      capture profiles, and radioactive tracing for per-client control-plane
      narrative.
    - Over-the-air capture: orchestrated conversion of a selected access point
      to sniffer mode with explicit band, channel and channel-width selection,
      and a built-in PEEKREMOTE listener to receive and store the 802.11 stream.
    - Explicit, prominent reporting of reduced coverage wherever a device,
      domain or switching mode cannot be captured.

4.4 Synchronized capture execution
    - Parallel pre-arming of all capture points across persistent SSH sessions.
    - Single-instant trigger with a target arming skew below 500 ms across all
      wired and wireless capture points, and below 100 ms on a local LAN.
    - Optional high-precision mode using device-side EEM or scheduled tasks
      triggered at an absolute NTP-synchronized epoch.
    - Coordinated start of stream-based captures (PEEKREMOTE, ERSPAN) alongside
      file-based captures (EPC, ethanalyzer) within the same capture window.

4.5 Safety, guardrails and hygiene
    - Mandatory capture filters; unfiltered NX-OS ethanalyzer captures refused.
    - Pre-flight health checks: CPU, memory, flash space, existing SPAN session
      count, controller redundancy state, and access point client count.
    - Service-impact gate for over-the-air capture: converting an access point
      to sniffer mode requires a separate, explicit, logged acknowledgement that
      the access point will leave service and disconnect its clients; the tool
      must report the current client count and recommend the least-impactful
      candidate access point.
    - Hard caps on duration, buffer size and packet count; headers-only snap
      length by default.
    - Device-side dead-man timer to force-stop captures and remove capture state
      if the controlling session is lost.
    - Guaranteed access point mode restoration, including recovery on
      application restart after a crash, with verification that the access point
      has rejoined the controller and returned to service.
    - Detection of Catalyst 9800 HA SSO switchover during a capture, treated as
      a session-invalidating event with clear user notification.
    - Post-run configuration and state diff to verify zero residual
      configuration and zero residual sniffer-mode access points.
    - Change-ticket capture, lawful-capture consent acknowledgement, and an
      immutable local audit log.

4.6 Collection, correlation and output
    - Secure retrieval of capture artifacts (SCP pull) with checksum
      verification and on-device cleanup.
    - Built-in ERSPAN (GRE 0x88BE) collector, PEEKREMOTE collector, and
      optional local NIC capture.
    - Normalization to pcapng with per-interface metadata recording device,
      interface or radio, capture domain, mechanism, and computed clock offset.
    - Automatic CAPWAP decapsulation and correlation of inner client flows to
      their outer tunnel, with both views retained.
    - Clock alignment using injected beacon frames plus RTT-compensated device
      clock reads.
    - Merge, de-duplication and hop-by-hop packet correlation across wired,
      CAPWAP and over-the-air domains.
    - Ladder-diagram timeline showing the full client journey, per-hop latency,
      and drop-point localization, including 802.11 association and
      authentication exchange state.
    - Correlation of radioactive trace output against captured packets on a
      single timeline.
    - Exportable evidence report and Wireshark-compatible artifacts.

5. SCOPE - OUT OF SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Continuous or always-on monitoring; this is an on-demand diagnostic tool.
- Intrusion detection, NDR, wireless intrusion prevention or security analytics.
- Replacement for a dedicated packet broker or TAP aggregation infrastructure.
- Spectrum analysis, RF interference classification or radio resource
  management tuning.
- Wireless site survey, coverage prediction or capacity planning.
- Guaranteed line-rate, lossless capture; on-box capture mechanisms are
  punt-path rate limited by design and this limitation must be surfaced.
- Legacy AireOS wireless controllers in the initial releases.
- Non-Cisco network operating systems in the initial releases.
- Replacement of Wireshark as an analysis front end.

6. TARGET USERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Network operations and NOC engineers performing live fault isolation.
- Wireless engineers diagnosing client onboarding, roaming and performance
  complaints.
- TAC and Technical Consulting engineers gathering evidence for escalation.
- Network architects and validation engineers verifying designs and changes.
- Security and compliance teams performing authorized traffic inspection.

7. SUCCESS CRITERIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SC-1  Discover and correctly classify at least 95 percent of reachable devices
      across all supported Cisco platform families, including controllers and
      access points, in a reference topology.
SC-2  Given a wireless client MAC address, correctly resolve the associated
      access point, radio, band, controller, WLAN, switching mode, VLAN and
      wired uplink in at least 95 percent of cases.
SC-3  Arm and trigger simultaneous captures on at least 20 devices spanning at
      least two capture domains, with measured arming skew below 500 ms.
SC-4  Produce a single merged, time-aligned pcapng whose cross-device timestamp
      alignment error is below 10 ms after beacon-based correction, including
      alignment between over-the-air and wired captures.
SC-5  Automatically decapsulate CAPWAP and correctly associate at least 99
      percent of inner client flows with their outer tunnel.
SC-6  Zero residual capture configuration on any device, and zero access points
      remaining in sniffer mode, after normal completion, user abort,
      application crash, or network disconnection - verified by automated
      configuration and state diff.
SC-7  No device control-plane CPU sustained above an operator-defined threshold
      as a direct result of a tool-initiated capture.
SC-8  Correctly identify the fault location in at least 90 percent of seeded
      fault scenarios in the validation lab, including at least ten wireless
      onboarding and roaming failure scenarios.
SC-9  Reduce end-to-end time from "problem reported" to "correlated evidence in
      hand" from hours to under 15 minutes for a 10-device path and under 20
      minutes for a full three-domain wireless client capture.
SC-10 Signed and installable artifacts for Windows (.exe), macOS (notarized
      .dmg) and Ubuntu (.deb and AppImage) produced from a single CI pipeline.

8. KEY CONSTRAINTS AND ASSUMPTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-1  Capture command syntax, buffer limits and feature availability vary by
     platform AND software release; capability data must be release-aware and
     regression-tested against captured golden CLI output.
C-2  NX-OS has no data-plane equivalent of EPC; ERSPAN or SPAN plumbing is
     mandatory for Nexus data-plane visibility and may be restricted by customer
     security policy.
C-3  Several classic Catalyst Layer 2 platforms do not support EPC at all.
C-4  Over-the-air 802.11 capture is inherently service-affecting: an access
     point placed in sniffer mode leaves service and disconnects its clients.
     This is the highest-impact action the tool can take and must be gated,
     consented, logged and automatically reverted.
C-5  Over-the-air capture is band, channel and channel-width specific. A sniffer
     access point observes only the channel it is tuned to, and cannot decrypt
     protected client data frames without the relevant key material. These
     limitations must be stated to the user before capture, not after.
C-6  Under FlexConnect local switching and SD-Access fabric-enabled wireless,
     client data traffic does not traverse the Catalyst 9800. The tool must
     detect the switching mode and redirect the capture plan to the correct
     enforcement point rather than reporting an empty capture.
C-7  Catalyst 9800 controllers may operate as HA SSO pairs. Captures are valid
     only on the active chassis, and a switchover mid-capture invalidates the
     session.
C-8  Access point capture behaviour depends on the AP model, its join state and
     the controller release; unsupported combinations must be detected during
     capability probing rather than failing at capture time.
C-9  The tool requires authenticated management access with sufficient privilege
     and must never store credentials in plaintext; OS-native secure storage
     (Windows DPAPI, macOS Keychain, Linux libsecret) is required.
C-10 Devices are assumed to be NTP-synchronized; the tool must still function,
     with degraded and clearly disclosed precision, when they are not.
C-11 Packet payloads may contain sensitive or personal data. Over-the-air
     capture additionally collects frames from clients and devices that are not
     the intended target and that may not belong to the operator. Headers-only
     capture is the default; full-payload and over-the-air capture must be
     explicit, logged and authorized actions.
C-12 Packet capture, and any change to an access point operating mode,
     constitute configuration changes on production infrastructure and must be
     treated as such by change management.

9. PRIMARY RISKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R-1  Cisco CLI drift across releases breaking drivers or parsers.
     Mitigation: version-pinned capability registry plus golden-output
     regression suite in CI.
R-2  Punt-path rate limiting causing incomplete captures to be misread as
     network packet loss.
     Mitigation: read and prominently display device-side drop and truncation
     counters alongside every capture.
R-3  Guardrail failure impacting a production control plane.
     Mitigation: mandatory filters, pre-flight health gates, hard caps,
     device-side dead-man timers, and staged lab-then-pilot rollout.
R-4  Residual configuration left on production devices, or an access point left
     out of service in sniffer mode - a direct and visible user-facing outage.
     Mitigation: journaled state machine, device-side auto-cleanup, crash-time
     recovery of pending mode restorations, and a mandatory post-run
     configuration and state diff assertion before a job is marked complete.
R-5  Customer security policy blocking the ERSPAN or PEEKREMOTE stream paths
     required for Nexus data-plane and over-the-air capture.
     Mitigation: explicit degraded-coverage mode with clear disclosure of what
     was not captured and why.
R-6  Misleading results in FlexConnect or fabric-enabled wireless deployments
     where the controller is not in the data path.
     Mitigation: mandatory switching-mode detection before plan generation, and
     refusal to present an empty controller capture as evidence of packet loss.
R-7  Over-the-air capture on the wrong channel or during a client roam yielding
     no useful frames while still having taken an access point out of service.
     Mitigation: pre-capture channel confirmation from live client state,
     multi-AP or multi-channel capture option, and a clear pre-flight summary of
     exactly what will and will not be observable.
R-8  Legal and privacy exposure from collecting third-party wireless traffic.
     Mitigation: explicit lawful-capture consent, headers-only default,
     immutable audit log, and configurable retention limits on stored captures.

10. DEFINITION OF DONE (INITIAL RELEASE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A network engineer can: enter one or more seed devices or a controller and
credentials; have the wired and wireless topology discovered, including per-WLAN
switching mode and access point placement; express a capture intent either as a
path (source, destination, protocol, port, duration) or as a single wireless
client MAC address; review an automatically generated per-device capture plan
stating the mechanism chosen for each capture domain, the estimated blast radius,
the explicit service impact of any over-the-air capture, and any coverage gaps;
execute a single synchronized capture across all selected devices and domains;
and receive a merged, time-aligned pcapng with CAPWAP already decapsulated, plus
a report that states where in the wireless or wired path the traffic was lost,
delayed, rejected or altered - with a verified guarantee that no configuration
was left behind on any device and that every access point has returned to
service.
